# Hemorrhoids / 痔疮 collection guide

Use this reference when the user asks for hemorrhoid-related TCM herb or formula collection.

## Safe framing

- Present collected content as **reference records**, not as a ranked medical prescription.
- State that hemorrhoids require colorectal evaluation when bleeding, pain, prolapse, infection, or recurrence is present.
- Do not provide a self-treatment dosage plan. If the source includes grams, store them as source metadata only when licensed/authorized and mark `clinician_review_required`.

## Ten commonly documented herbal approaches

1. **黄柏**: clear heat and dry dampness; common in damp-heat itching, swelling, and external wash records.
2. **槐花/槐角**: cool blood and stop bleeding; common in bright-red bleeding and Huai Jiao Wan-style records.
3. **蒲公英**: clear heat, resolve toxicity, reduce swelling; common in inflamed swelling and wash combinations.
4. **白芷**: reduce swelling, discharge pus, relieve pain; common in external swelling/pain references.
5. **苦参**: clear heat, dry dampness, relieve itching; common in sitz bath and topical records.
6. **当归**: nourish/move blood and moisten intestines; common when deficiency, stasis, or constipation is discussed.
7. **桃仁**: move blood, dispel stasis, moisten intestines; high caution for pregnancy and bleeding risks.
8. **赤芍**: clear heat, cool blood, disperse stasis; common in heat-stasis swelling/pain wash formulas.
9. **牡丹皮/丹皮**: clear heat, cool blood, move stasis; common with 赤芍/黄柏/蒲公英 combinations.
10. **土茯苓**: resolve dampness, detoxify, reduce swelling; common in damp-heat external wash records.

## Formula strategy notes

- External wash/fumigation records often combine 蒲公英、黄柏、赤芍、丹皮、桃仁、土茯苓、白芷.
- Bleeding-control records often include 槐花/槐角、地榆、黄芩、防风、枳壳.
- Constipation/stasis records may include 当归、桃仁、火麻仁/大麻仁, but internal use must be clinician-reviewed.

## Red flags

Advise medical care for heavy bleeding, dizziness/fainting, black stool, dark-red blood, recurrent bleeding, persistent severe pain, fever, suspected perianal abscess, incarcerated hemorrhoids, unexplained weight loss, or new rectal bleeding in middle-aged/older adults.
